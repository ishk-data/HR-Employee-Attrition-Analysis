# Business Insights and Recommendations

## HR Employee Attrition Analysis

### Executive Summary

This analysis examined employee attrition across **1,470 employees** to identify workforce segments with higher employee turnover.

The overall attrition rate was **16.12%**, with **237 employees leaving**.

The analysis identified several areas with notably higher attrition, including early-tenure employees, younger employees, employees working overtime, selected job roles, and frequent business travelers.

These findings can help HR teams prioritize retention strategies and workforce planning efforts.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Overall Attrition

* Total Employees: **1,470**
* Employees Left: **237**
* Overall Attrition Rate: **16.12%**

The organization has a 16.12% overall employee attrition rate, providing a baseline for evaluating higher-risk workforce segments.

---

### 2. Tenure and Attrition

| Tenure Group      | Employees | Employees Left | Attrition Rate |
| ----------------- | --------: | -------------: | -------------: |
| Less Than 2 Years |       215 |             75 |         34.88% |
| 2-4 Years         |       365 |             66 |         18.08% |
| 5-9 Years         |       524 |             58 |         11.07% |
| 10-14 Years       |       208 |             24 |         11.54% |
| 15+ Years         |       158 |             14 |          8.86% |

**Insight:** Employees with less than 2 years of tenure have the highest attrition rate at **34.88%**.

**Recommendation:** Strengthen onboarding, mentoring, employee check-ins, and career development programs during the first two years of employment.

---

### 3. Age and Attrition

| Age Group | Employees | Employees Left | Attrition Rate |
| --------- | --------: | -------------: | -------------: |
| Under 25  |        97 |             38 |         39.18% |
| 25-34     |       554 |            112 |         20.22% |
| 35-44     |       505 |             51 |         10.10% |
| 45-54     |       245 |             25 |         10.20% |
| 55+       |        69 |             11 |         15.94% |

**Insight:** Employees under 25 have the highest attrition rate at **39.18%**.

**Recommendation:** Consider targeted mentoring, career development, training, and engagement programs for younger employees.

---

### 4. Overtime and Attrition

| Overtime | Employees | Employees Left | Attrition Rate |
| -------- | --------: | -------------: | -------------: |
| Yes      |       416 |            127 |         30.53% |
| No       |     1,054 |            110 |         10.44% |

**Insight:** Employees working overtime have an attrition rate of **30.53%**, compared with **10.44%** among employees who do not work overtime.

**Recommendation:** Monitor overtime levels, staffing requirements, workload distribution, and employee workload in teams with consistently high overtime.

### 6. Department and Attrition

---

### 7. Business Travel and Attrition

| Business Travel   | Employees | Employees Left | Attrition Rate |
| ----------------- | --------: | -------------: | -------------: |
| Non-Travel        |       150 |             12 |          8.00% |
| Travel_Rarely     |     1,043 |            156 |         14.96% |
| Travel_Frequently |       277 |             69 |         24.91% |

**Insight:** Employees who travel frequently have a **24.91%** attrition rate, compared with **8.00%** among employees who do not travel.

**Recommendation:** Review travel requirements, workload, scheduling, flexibility, and employee feedback for frequently traveling employees.

## Priority Areas for HR

Based on the analysis, the following areas should receive particular attention:

### High Priority

1. **Employees under 25** — 39.18% attrition
2. **Sales Representatives** — 39.76% attrition
3. **Employees with less than 2 years of tenure** — 34.88% attrition
4. **Employees working overtime** — 30.53% attrition
5. **Frequent business travelers** — 24.91% attrition

### Medium Priority

* Sales department — 20.63%
* Laboratory Technicians — 23.94%
* Human Resources roles — 23.08%
* Employees with 2–4 years of tenure — 18.08%

---

## Recommended HR Actions

### Short-Term Actions

* Review overtime patterns and workload.
* Conduct retention discussions with employees in high-risk groups.
* Strengthen new-hire onboarding and check-ins.
* Investigate attrition within Sales and high-risk job roles.
* Review employee feedback from frequently traveling employees.

### Medium-Term Actions

* Develop mentoring programs for newer employees.
* Create clearer career progression opportunities.
* Review compensation and benefits for high-attrition roles.
* Evaluate workload and staffing levels.
* Develop role-specific retention programs.

### Long-Term Actions

* Build automated HR attrition dashboards.
* Establish regular workforce analytics reporting.
* Develop predictive attrition models.
* Create employee risk segmentation.
* Monitor retention KPIs over time.

---

## Important Analytical Note

The findings in this project represent **associations observed in the employee data**. They do not establish that a specific factor directly causes employee attrition.

For example, the higher attrition rate among employees working overtime indicates an association between overtime and attrition, but additional analysis would be required to determine whether overtime directly contributes to employees leaving.

Future predictive and statistical analysis could be used to investigate these relationships in greater depth.

---

## Conclusion

The analysis shows that employee attrition is not evenly distributed across the workforce.

The strongest areas of concern include **younger employees, early-tenure employees, employees working overtime, Sales Representatives, and frequent business travelers**.

By focusing retention efforts on these segments, HR teams can prioritize resources where the analysis indicates greater attrition risk.
